Environmental data scientist · NCEAS, UC Santa Barbara

Hi, I'm Carlo

I make maps and data tools to explore how humanity and the natural environment interact. Right now that means wildfire: at the National Center for Ecological Analysis and Synthesis (NCEAS) I help build the Wildfire Resilience Index, a free tool that measures how well every community and landscape in the U.S. and Canadian West can resist wildfire and recover after it. I build the data pipelines and interactive maps that communicate wildfire science and help communities exposed to wildfire.

I was a Fulbright Scholar awarded an Open Study/Research Award in Asunción, Paraguay, from 2025 to 2026, and I still work with partners there on wildfire science. Before that I was an Ocean Health Index fellow, and I spent a few years in supply-chain sustainability and then plastics and plastics recycling.

NCEAS · 2023–present

Wildfire Resilience Index

A free, open-access tool that scores wildfire resilience from 0 to 100 for any community or landscape in the U.S. and Canadian West, across eight domains from infrastructure and communities to water and air. I build the data pipelines and interactive maps behind it.

Interactive map · 2026

Wildfire Evacuation Risk Across U.S. Communities

How many roads lead out of 30,728 small U.S. communities, set against how likely the land around them is to burn. Built from OpenStreetMap road networks and national burn-probability data.

Paper: Fong, Broderick, Moritz & Halpern, Egress thresholds and wildfire fatalities, PNAS (2026).

Paraguay · 2025–present

Parafire

A fire-risk, simulation and rapid-response platform for INFONA, Paraguay's national forestry institute, built with Forest4Life Paraguay. It includes a review of the country's weather-station data and a fire-spread simulator tested against historical fires.
