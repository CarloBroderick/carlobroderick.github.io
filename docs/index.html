Research Associate · NCEAS, UC Santa Barbara

Hi, I'm Carlo 👋

I make maps and data tools about wildfire and the places it touches. My day job is at the National Center for Ecological Analysis and Synthesis (NCEAS), where I help build the Wildfire Resilience Index: the data pipelines and interactive maps that show where communities are exposed to fire and how well they can cope.

I was a Fulbright Scholar in Asunción, Paraguay in 2025–26, and I still work with partners there on fire simulation. Before that I was an Ocean Health Index fellow, and I spent a few years in supply-chain sustainability, starting as an early employee at SupplyShift.

Wildfire Resilience Index

A look at how well communities in the western U.S. can resist, recover from and adapt to wildfire. I process and analyze the data behind its indicators, from road networks to burn hazard.

Interactive map · 2026

Wildfire Evacuation Risk Across U.S. Communities

How many roads lead out of 30,728 small U.S. communities, set against how likely the land around them is to burn. Built from OpenStreetMap road networks and national burn-probability data.

Paper: Fong, Broderick, Moritz & Halpern, Egress thresholds and wildfire fatalities, PNAS (2026).

Paraguay · 2025–present

Parafire

A fire-risk, simulation and rapid-response platform for INFONA, Paraguay's national forestry institute, built with Forest4Life Paraguay. It includes a review of the country's weather-station data and a fire-spread simulator tested against historical fires.
